Abstract

Evidence-grounded medical question answering requires not only accurate answers, but also precise, verifiable, and clinically trustworthy evidence. Existing medical RAG systems typically retrieve top-k chunks according to item-wise relevance. Although this design improves recall, it does not explicitly optimize whether the selected evidence set is compact, sufficient, and decision-critical.

We propose MEGA, a gain-aware framework for compact supporting medical evidence selection. MEGA expands clinical queries into complementary subqueries and performs hybrid retrieval to construct a recall-oriented candidate pool. Hidden-state Information Gain Scoring uses a frozen LLM to estimate whether each snippet contributes new answer-relevant information beyond surface relevance. An FPTAS selector then identifies a compact evidence subset under an explicit token budget. Across three LLM backbones and five medical RAG baselines on CRC-EvidenceQA and Med-EvidenceQA, the paper reports average relative improvements of 8.8% in answer quality and 38.8% in Evidence F1 over the second-best baseline. Ablation studies and blinded clinical expert evaluation further assess the framework.

Method

The pipeline combines subquery generation and hybrid retrieval, frozen-model Information Gain Scoring, budgeted FPTAS evidence selection, and evidence-grounded answer generation.

The hidden-state signals are evidence-utility proxies. The FPTAS approximation guarantee concerns the fixed additive selection objective; downstream answer quality and evidence grounding are evaluated empirically.

Benchmarks

CRC-EvidenceQA contains 200 open-ended, clinician-collaborative colorectal cancer questions: 100 Chinese and 100 English, with 50 low-difficulty and 50 high-difficulty questions per language. We evaluate answer quality and Evidence F1.

Med-EvidenceQA contains 1,000 yes/no/maybe examples adapted from PubMedQA and 1,973 extractive examples adapted from COVID-QA. It supports task-specific answer evaluation and evidence selection evaluation.
